Why Ringwood Drains Block More Than Most

A large share of Ringwood's sewers are older clay lines, and clay is the pipe material most prone to root intrusion. There is a specific historical reason Ringwood has so much of it.

Ringwood's sewerage scheme was planned in the 1920s but only got underway after the Second World War, and the Monash-based Victorian Places project describes "a long catch-up period as postwar housing quickly spread". The housing arrived fast — the Ringwood district's population went from 4,897 in 1947 to 34,751 by 1971 — and Maroondah City Council's own neighbourhood character study describes the residential core as "simple Post War era 1950s and 1960s weatherboard and brick, 1960s and 1970s L-shaped and 1980s adaptations of the L-shape form".

That timing matters. According to the Plastics Industry Pipe Association of Australia, PVC only entered the Australian house-connection market during the 1970s and did not become the predominant material until the 1980s. Houses plumbed before that were connected in vitrified clay — earthenware pipe laid in short lengths with a joint every metre or so. Every one of those joints is a potential way in.

🌳 The number that explains it: In root-intrusion trials involving CSIRO and reported by the plastics pipe industry, 7 of 8 vitrified clay pipe joints were penetrated by roots within 32 months. The PVC joints in the same trial showed no intrusions at all. That is the difference between a pre-1970s Ringwood sewer and a modern one.

It is worth being accurate about how roots actually get in, because it is often explained badly. Roots do not drill through sound pipe. Public guidance issued by a Victorian council puts it plainly: damage "more often arises from improper installation, poor maintenance, faulty joints, cracks, and/or ground movement which result in leaks that tree roots exploit, following the source of the soil moisture" — and this "typically occurs in old style earthenware clay pipes". It is, in the council's words, "extremely unlikely that tree roots will result in damage to intact pipes".

So a root-blocked drain in Ringwood is telling you two things: there are roots, and there was already a fault for them to find. Clearing the roots without finding the fault is why so many drains reblock the following season.

What to Do Right Now

If your Ringwood drain is backing up, these five steps limit the damage before a plumber arrives. The most important one is the first.

  1. Stop using water everywhere in the house. That includes the washing machine and dishwasher — a wash cycle mid-blockage is what pushes sewage up through a shower base.
  2. Do not keep flushing. Flushing a blocked toilet again to "see if it clears" is the most common way a blockage becomes a clean-up.
  3. Go and look at the overflow relief gully. It is the grated drain outside, set lower than your floor level. If it is spilling, it is doing its job — releasing sewage outside instead of inside. Keep it clear and do not block it off.
  4. Skip the chemical drain cleaner. It rarely shifts a root or grease blockage, and it leaves caustic liquid sitting in the line that a plumber then has to work in.
  5. Note what changed. Which drain went first, whether it followed heavy rain, and whether it has happened before. That history often identifies the cause before the camera goes in.

When Ringwood Drains Block — The Seasonal Pattern

Blocked drain calls in Ringwood are not spread evenly through the year, and knowing the pattern helps you catch problems early.

🍂 Autumn

Leaf fall from Ringwood's established canopy fills gutters, downpipes and stormwater pits. This is the time to clear them, before the first real downpour tests the system.

🌧️ Winter

Heavy rain finds every partially blocked stormwater line. Maroondah's residential network is designed only to a 5 year ARI standard, so a half-blocked drain shows up fast as water in the yard.

🌱 Spring

Root growth accelerates. A sewer line cleared last winter frequently reblocks now if the joint the roots came through was never sealed.

☀️ Summer

Dry ground pushes roots deeper toward the moisture leaking from a cracked sewer, and grease sets harder in kitchen lines during long dry spells.

Who Is Responsible for a Blocked Drain in Ringwood — You, Yarra Valley Water, or the Council?

This is the question Ringwood homeowners get wrong most often, and getting it wrong costs money. There are three different parties depending on which pipe has blocked.

🚽 Sewer — Yarra Valley Water

Yarra Valley Water is the sewerage authority for Ringwood, confirmed by Maroondah City Council's own drainage guidance. The dividing line is a fitting called the Inspection Opening, sometimes called a 27A, usually near the property boundary.

🌧️ Stormwater — Maroondah Council

Maroondah states property owners are responsible "up to and including the point of discharge to Council's stormwater drainage system". A blocked Council drain is reported to Maroondah; a blocked private drain, in the council's own words, means "contact a plumber".

🏠 Everything inside the boundary — you

Yarra Valley Water is explicit that it "is not responsible for the maintenance of internal pipes and will not provide a reimbursement for internal pipe repairs". The overflow relief gully, vents and traps are the owner's responsibility too.

Renting in Ringwood? As a general rule the owner covers the drain itself — it is part of the property — while a tenant can be charged if the blockage was caused by what went down it, such as wipes, nappies or cooking fat. If you are a tenant, report it to the agent in writing and do not authorise works yourself. If you are a landlord or property manager, a camera inspection is what settles the argument, because it shows whether the cause was roots and a failed pipe or something flushed. Our guide on who is responsible for a blocked drain covers it in more detail.

One important caveat. It is tempting to say responsibility simply ends at the property boundary, but Yarra Valley Water does not put it that way. Its position is that responsibility "is dependent on the location of the sewer main and the first Inspection Opening", with different scenarios depending on whether the sewer main runs outside your property, inside it, or serves several properties through combined private plumbing. If you are not sure which applies, we can identify the connection point on site.

⚠️ Worth knowing before you call the water authority: Yarra Valley Water states that if you call them to clear a blockage and the inspection shaft or boundary trap is buried, the crew "cannot clear the blockage without it being at surface level" — and raising it is the property owner's job. Plenty of Ringwood homes have had a garden bed, lawn or paving grow over that point. We can locate and raise it.

There is also a drainage reality worth understanding. The Maroondah Flood Management Plan notes that the municipality's residential stormwater network is designed only to a 5 year ARI standard, and that storm runoff "causes local flooding in properties located in the low pockets due to blockage of flow paths". In heavy rain a Ringwood stormwater system can be overwhelmed even when nothing is blocked — but a partially blocked line turns an inconvenience into water in the yard.

Signs Your Ringwood Drain Is Blocked

Some of these are obvious. Others get lived with for months before anyone connects them to a drain problem.

  • Gurgling from several drains at once — air being pushed back through the traps because wastewater cannot get past. Our guide on why drains gurgle explains the mechanism.
  • Water rising in the shower when you flush the toilet — a classic main-line symptom, because the toilet and shower share the same pipe.
  • The outside grate overflowing — that is your overflow relief gully doing its job, releasing sewage outside so it does not come up through your floor. Treat it as urgent.
  • A sewage smell in the yard or bathroom — see why drains smell.
  • One drain that keeps blocking every few months — the tell-tale sign of root intrusion through a joint, very common in Ringwood's older streets.
  • A soggy patch of lawn that never dries out — often a cracked or leaking sewer line below.
  • A slow kitchen sink — usually grease hardening in the branch line. See kitchen sink draining slowly.

Repeat Blockages: When Ringwood Pipes Need Relining, Not Clearing

If a Ringwood drain has blocked more than once in the same spot, clearing it again is buying time rather than fixing it. Roots regrow through the same opening, usually within a season or two.

Relining is a trenchless repair — it seals the pipe from the inside. A resin liner is cured in place against the existing pipe wall, closing the joints the roots came through and leaving a smooth continuous surface, without digging up the garden, driveway or paving. This is not just a plumber's opinion. Victorian council guidance advises that pipes which continually block from root infiltration "should be repaired or replaced with PVC pipes to prevent recurrence", noting that "alternatively, it may also be possible to reline existing earthenware pipes".

Given how much of Ringwood was plumbed in clay before the 1970s, relining is frequently the right long-term answer here. See pipe relining and our guide on relining versus replacement.
