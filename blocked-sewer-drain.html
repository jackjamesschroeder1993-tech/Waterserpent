Blocked Sewer Specialists Not Just Any Plumber

A blocked sewer is different from a single blocked drain. When the main sewer line backs up it affects the whole house — and clearing it properly takes the right equipment, not a hand-held drain snake. Signs You Have a Blocked Sewer (Not Just a Blocked Drain)

🏠 Multiple fixtures affected at once

Toilet, shower and sink all draining slowly or backing up together — the blockage is in the main line, not one pipe.

🫧 Gurgling and rising water

The toilet gurgles or the water level moves when you run the sink or washing machine.

🤢 Sewage smell or overflow

A foul smell inside or near the outside gully trap, or sewage pooling at the lowest drain or in the yard.

🚨 Sewage backing up

Waste coming back up the lowest fixture (often a ground-floor shower or floor waste) when you flush.

What Causes a Blocked Sewer in Melbourne

🌳 Tree roots — the #1 cause

Established gums and large trees across the Dandenong Ranges and Yarra Valley send roots into older sewer joints chasing moisture, then choke the line.

🏺 Old clay / earthenware pipes

Common in established homes; they crack and offset with age, letting roots in and waste out.

🕳️ Collapsed or misaligned pipe

Ground movement and tree pressure can crush or shift a section of sewer.

🍳 Fat, grease and flushed wipes

Build up over time and snag on any rough joint or root.

🧸 Foreign objects

Anything that shouldn't be flushed eventually lodges in the main.

No-Dig Sewer Repair (Pipe Relining)

If your sewer keeps blocking, the pipe itself is usually the problem — cracked, offset or root-infested. Digging it up means tearing through lawns, paths and driveways. Instead we use trenchless pipe relining: a new structural liner is cured inside the old pipe, sealing out roots and restoring full flow — typically in a day, with no excavation. It's the long-term answer to a sewer that blocks again and again.

Who's Responsible — You or the Water Authority?

As a rule of thumb, the property owner is responsible for the sewer pipes within the property boundary (the house drain and the connection up to the boundary), while the water authority looks after the main sewer in the street. Most blockages we attend — tree roots, cracked house drains, gully-trap overflows — sit on the property side, which is why they fall to the owner. If a camera shows the issue is past the boundary, we'll tell you and point you to the right authority. Either way, the CCTV footage gives you proof of exactly where the problem is.
