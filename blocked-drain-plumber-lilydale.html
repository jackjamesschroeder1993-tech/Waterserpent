Lilydale Is Not a Standard Melbourne Suburb

Most suburbs east of the city are a single story: farmland, then a wave of subdivision, then piped services throughout. Lilydale is not that. It is a township that has been here since the survey of 1860, that stayed a country town far longer than its neighbours, and that in parts was still on septic tanks in the 2020s. That mixture is exactly why drainage problems here do not follow one pattern.

The Monash-based Victorian Places project records that "Lilydale remained a country town in a mainly rural landscape until the 1970s", and that "after the 1980s Lilydale's residential areas expanded to beyond walking distance to the station". The census numbers tell the same story bluntly — 212 people in 1871, 2,199 in 1947, 9,502 in 1991, 15,649 by 2011 and 17,348 at the 2021 count. The suburb roughly doubled in the twenty years to 2011, so a very large share of the housing here went in between the 1980s and the 2000s, wrapped around a small nineteenth-century core.

For a plumber that means one thing above all: you cannot assume what is under a Lilydale property. Two houses in the same street can be twenty or forty years apart in the age of their drainage, in the material it was laid in, and even in whether it goes to a sewer main or to a tank in the back yard. Guessing is expensive. A camera in the line is not.

🏘️ Worth knowing: Main Street is the Maroondah Highway through the middle of town, and Lilydale station is the terminus of the Lilydale line, rebuilt in November 2021 with the rail raised above the highway. If you are around the Marketplace, Lilydale Village or the service lanes off Main Street, you are on some of the busiest and oldest drainage in the township.

Septic Tanks in Lilydale — and the Changeover to Sewer

This is the fact that separates Lilydale from every other suburb we cover, and it catches people out constantly. Parts of Lilydale were unsewered until very recently. The suburb is one of only six areas included in Yarra Valley Water's Community Sewerage Program, the scheme that brings piped sewerage to established neighbourhoods that never got it.

Under the Lilydale Sewerage Project, works targeting unsewered parts of south Lilydale were completed in September 2025, letting almost 200 Lilydale households move off septic tanks and onto piped sewerage. Yarra Valley Water funded the network itself and the connection point brought to each property; what residents pay for is the work on their own land to reach it. Yarra Valley Water is blunt about why the program exists — poorly performing septic systems cause odour and, in its words, "soggy backyards". Lilydale is served by a hybrid arrangement of gravity and pressure sewerage, so not every connection looks the same.

Practically, there are three situations we get called to in Lilydale:

  • Still on a septic system. Slow fixtures, gurgling, smell, or a wet patch over the trench usually means the outlet line or the absorption field is failing rather than the house drain. We clear and camera the line to the tank so you know which it is before you spend anything. See blocked septics.
  • Changing over to sewer. Once the connection point is at your boundary, the plumbing on your side is a proper job — new drainage set to grade, the old tank decommissioned, and the fixtures rerouted. It is also the single best opportunity you will ever get to replace tired pipework while the ground is already open.
  • Recently connected. New sewer, old house drains. If the internal lines were reused, a blockage after changeover is very often in the original pipework rather than in anything the project touched.

If a neighbour's property is on sewer and yours is not, that is entirely normal in this part of the township. The rollout was staged street by street.

What to Do Right Now

While you wait for us, these five steps keep a Lilydale blockage from becoming a clean-up job. Do the first one immediately.

  1. Turn off every water-using appliance. The washing machine and dishwasher are the culprits people forget — a full cycle emptying into a blocked line is what forces wastewater up through the lowest fixture in the house.
  2. Leave the toilet alone. Another flush to test it is the single most common reason a contained blockage ends up on the bathroom floor.
  3. Walk outside and find the grated drain. Your overflow relief gully sits below floor level for a reason. If it is releasing, it is protecting the inside of your house. Clear anything sitting over it and never seal it up.
  4. Leave the drain chemicals on the shelf. They do very little to roots or set grease, and they leave a caustic pool in the line that has to be dealt with before anyone can work in it safely.
  5. Write down the sequence. Which fixture went first, whether it followed a downpour, whether the same thing happened last year. On a Lilydale property, also note whether you are on sewer or still on a tank — it changes where we start.

When Lilydale Drains Block — The Seasonal Pattern

Our Lilydale callouts cluster in fairly predictable ways through the year. Knowing which season is coming lets you get ahead of it.

🍂 Autumn

Leaf litter loads up gutters, downpipes and pits. On streets with open table drains rather than kerb and channel, the channel silts up as well — worth a rake before the first serious rain.

🌧️ Winter

Sustained wet weather saturates the ground. Septic absorption fields have nowhere to send effluent, so tank-served properties get their worst symptoms now, and any restricted stormwater line shows itself as standing water.

🌱 Spring

Roots put on growth and go looking for moisture. Last winter's clear will not hold if nobody sealed the fracture those roots were using, and spring is when that shows.

☀️ Summer

Dry ground drives roots deeper toward whatever is leaking, and the storms that arrive on a hot afternoon are the intense short bursts Lilydale's gutters cope with worst.

Who Is Responsible for a Blocked Drain in Lilydale — You, Yarra Valley Water, or Yarra Ranges Council?

Lilydale sits in the Shire of Yarra Ranges, and the council's head office is in the suburb itself at 15 Anderson Street. That closeness does not mean the council owns your drain. Three parties are involved, and which one applies depends on the pipe.

🚽 Sewer — Yarra Valley Water

Lilydale's sewerage authority is Yarra Valley Water, on 13 27 62. Worth noting: the Yarra Ranges municipality is split between two retailers, so a neighbouring town may be South East Water — but Lilydale itself is Yarra Valley Water.

🌧️ Stormwater — Yarra Ranges Council

The council is the Local Drainage Authority under the Water Act 1989 and runs the public stormwater network. Report a council drain on 1300 368 333. Anything on your side of the boundary is a plumbing job, not a council one.

🏠 Up to the legal point of discharge — you

Yarra Ranges puts it in writing: "The landowners, developers, and body corporates are the responsible parties up to the Legal Point of Discharge, excluding cases where the asset falls within a drainage easement."

The phrase to learn is "legal point of discharge". It is the specific spot where your private drainage is permitted to hand over to something public, and it marks the end of your responsibility. What makes Yarra Ranges different from the councils further down the highway is what that point can actually be. The council recognises it as "usually an underground drain, kerbs or open table drains" — those open grassed channels beside the road that you still see across plenty of Lilydale. No metropolitan council uses that wording, and it is a fair reminder that this is a semi-rural township with semi-rural drainage in places.

Two things Lilydale owners are frequently surprised by. First, the council states its drainage network is managed "largely on a reactive basis in response to enquiries, requests, and complaints" — so nothing gets looked at until someone reports it. Second, the council is clear that it "is not designated under this legislation to enforce nuisance flooding compliance between two private properties". If your neighbour's runoff is coming across your block, that is a civil matter between you, not something the council will step in and resolve.

Renting in Lilydale? The drain is a fixture of the property, so repairs sit with the owner as a general rule, though an occupier can be billed where whatever stopped the pipe plainly went in from inside the house. Put your report to the property manager in writing so there is a dated record of it, and leave the ordering of any works to them. Owners and managers get their answer from the camera run: it shows either a pipe that has failed or an object that had no business being there. Olinda Creek, Lillydale Lake and the Streets That Flood

Lilydale drains into its own catchment, and it is not the one that serves the suburbs closer to the city. Olinda Creek rises near Mount Dandenong, runs down through Lilydale, feeds Lillydale Lake, then continues north to meet the Yarra River at Yering. That lake is not only a park — it functions as a flood retarding basin for the area, with the reservoir and wetlands managed by Melbourne Water and the parkland by Yarra Ranges Council.

The VICSES Lilydale Local Flood Guide names the streets that suffer, and locals will recognise most of them: Swansea Road, Cave Hill Road, Quarry Road, Beresford Road, Hull Road, Chapel Street, John Street, Nelson Road, William Street East and the Main Street service lanes. Flooding has been recorded in the district as far back as 1891. On 7 February 2002 Lilydale took a month's rainfall in two hours, houses were flooded and roads closed. Storms in 2011 closed the Melba Highway. On 29 December 2016, 50 mm fell in 16 hours with 15 mm of it in a single ten-minute burst, overwhelming gutters and putting water across Beresford and Hereford Roads and the Maroondah Highway.

None of that is caused by a blocked drain. But if you live on one of those streets, a partially restricted stormwater line is the difference between a wet kerb and water in the garage. Getting pits, downpipe lines and table drain outlets clear before winter is cheap insurance — see our stormwater health check.

Signs Your Lilydale Drain Is Blocked

A few of these are obvious. Others get tolerated for a year before anyone links them to drainage.

  • Several drains gurgling in sequence — air forced back through the traps because wastewater cannot get away. Our guide on why drains gurgle explains it.
  • The shower base filling when the toilet is flushed — they share a line, and something downstream of both is restricting it.
  • Water coming out of the outside grate — the overflow relief gully releasing outside so it does not come up indoors. Urgent.
  • Odour around the yard or bathroom — see why drains smell.
  • A patch of lawn that stays green and soft through a dry spell — on a sewered property that suggests a leaking drain; on a septic property it may be the absorption field failing.
  • The same fixture blocking every year at roughly the same time — seasonal root growth through an existing defect.
  • A kitchen sink that has been slow for months — usually fat narrowing the branch line. See kitchen sink draining slowly.

Repeat Blockages: When a Lilydale Line Needs Relining, Not Clearing

If the same length of pipe has blocked twice, clearing it a third time is renting a solution rather than buying one. Whatever got in the first time has an opening to come back through, and it will.

Relining repairs the pipe from the inside, with no excavation. A resin-soaked sleeve is inverted through the bad length and left to set, sealing off cracks and open joints and leaving a single unbroken bore where a chain of jointed sections used to be. Nothing is dug up, which matters on the larger blocks common in Lilydale where a sewer run can be long and pass under landscaping you would rather not lose.

Given how varied the pipework is across this township, whether relining is warranted depends entirely on what the camera shows — and we will say so when clearing is genuinely enough. See pipe relining and our comparison of relining versus replacement.
