What Is an Overflow Relief Gully?

Every modern Melbourne home has one: a grated drain sitting at ground level outside, usually near the laundry, bathroom or kitchen wall. Most people never give it a second look — until the day it erupts.

The overflow relief gully (ORG) is a deliberate weak point built into your sewer system. It sits lower than every drain inside your house. When the main sewer line blocks and wastewater starts backing up, the pressure pops the ORG's loose grate and the backup spills out into the yard — before it can reach the lowest toilet, shower or floor waste inside. No ORG (or a blocked one), and that same sewage comes up through your bathroom floor instead. Given the choice between a puddle in the yard and a flooded hallway, you want the puddle every time.

Why It's Overflowing — and What to Do Right Now

An overflowing ORG means one thing: the sewer line between your house and the street is blocked, and everything you flush or drain is now taking the emergency exit. Here's the drill:

1️⃣ Stop using water

No flushing, no showers, no washing machine, no dishwasher. Every litre you send down a drain right now comes out of that gully — or worse, backs up inside.

2️⃣ Keep kids and pets away

What's spilling is raw sewage — a genuine health hazard. Rope off the area and don't hose it toward garden beds or the veggie patch.

3️⃣ Don't reach for chemicals

Caustic cleaners won't touch a main-line blockage — they just make the pipe more hazardous to work on. Skip them.

4️⃣ Call a drain plumber

A main sewer blockage needs a jetter or drain machine and a camera to find the cause. This is exactly what our blocked sewer service does, same-day.

In the Dandenong Ranges and Melbourne's east, the cause is almost always tree roots that have worked into old earthenware pipe joints — with wipes, grease and the occasional collapsed pipe section making up the rest. The fix is high-pressure water jetting to cut the roots out and scour the line, with a CCTV camera run afterwards so you can see exactly what caused it — and whether it'll happen again.

Bubbling, Smelly or Damp — the Early Warnings

A full eruption is the end of the story. The ORG usually drops hints first, and catching them early is the difference between a routine clear and an after-hours emergency:

Gurgling or bubbling from the gully when the washing machine drains or a toilet flushes means air is being forced past a partial blockage — the line is choking but not yet closed. A sewage smell near the gully means either a dried-out water trap (tip a bucket of water in to refill it) or waste sitting in a partially blocked pipe. Water rising in the gully and slowly draining away means the blockage is nearly complete. Any of these alongside gurgling drains inside is your sewer line waving a red flag — a camera inspection now beats a sewage spill later.

The Covered ORG Problem — Check Yours Today

Here's the one I see all the time on older properties in Lilydale, Mooroolbark and Ferntree Gully: the ORG has been paved over, buried under a garden bed, hidden under pot plants, or had its grate glued or screwed down during a reno. A covered ORG can't pop. Which means when the sewer blocks, the pressure relief valve is welded shut — and the sewage takes the next available exit: your shower, your floor waste, your loo.

Two minutes today can save you a ruined floor: find your ORG (grated drain, near a wet-area wall, outside), and check that the grate sits loose, sits proud of the surrounding garden, and has nothing stacked on top. If yours is buried, sealed or sitting below garden level, it's a small job to fix — and one insurance assessors ask about after an inside sewage flood.

Cleared vs Fixed — Don't Pay Twice

Clearing the blockage gets your drains flowing today. But if the camera shows root-cracked or collapsed pipe underneath, the roots grow straight back through the same entry point — usually within months. That's why every main-line job I do ends with a camera run: you see the pipe's condition on screen, and if it needs a permanent fix, trenchless pipe relining repairs it from the inside without digging up the yard. Frequently Asked Questions

An overflow relief gully (ORG) is the grated drain sitting at ground level outside your house, usually near the laundry, bathroom or kitchen wall. It's a deliberate safety valve on your sewer line: if the sewer blocks, the ORG is designed to pop its grate and spill wastewater outside in the yard — instead of letting raw sewage back up through your toilets, showers and floor wastes inside the house.

If the grated drain outside your house is overflowing with dirty water or sewage, your main sewer line is blocked and the overflow relief gully is doing its job — releasing the backup outside before it comes up inside. In Melbourne's east the cause is usually tree roots in the line. Stop using water (no flushing, no showers, no washing machine) and call a drain plumber — this needs machine clearing, not a plunger.

Treat it as urgent. The ORG overflowing means your sewer is fully or nearly fully blocked, and the next stop for that sewage is inside your house. You can clear leaves or dirt off the grate, and if the gully itself is full of debris you can scoop it out — a jammed or buried grate stops the ORG doing its safety job. But if wastewater is coming UP out of the gully, the blockage is downstream in the sewer line and no amount of cleaning the gully will fix it. That needs a plumber with a jetter or drain machine, and a camera to find the cause.

A smelly ORG usually means the water trap under it has dried out or the line is partially blocked and sewage is sitting in the pipe. Tip a bucket of water into the gully to refill the trap. If the smell keeps coming back, or you notice gurgling and slow drains as well, there's a partial sewer blockage building — worth a camera inspection before it becomes a full backup.

The grate should sit loose (not screwed, glued or paved over), at least 150mm below the lowest indoor drain and clear of garden beds, pots and paving. Nothing should cover it — a covered ORG can't relieve pressure, which forces a sewer backup inside your house.
