What Actually Drives the Price

Two jetting jobs are rarely identical, and a good plumber prices the job in front of them, not a one-size-fits-all number. Here's what moves the figure up or down:

🚿 One fixture or the main line

Clearing a single slow sink is a smaller job than a backed-up main line serving the whole house. The more of the system that's affected, the more there is to do.

🌳 How bad the blockage is

A light grease film clears fast. A main line packed solid with tree roots takes longer and more passes with the cutting head.

📏 Pipe length and access

An easy inspection-point near the house is quick. A long run, or a drain that's hard to get to, adds time — and time is what you're really paying for.

📷 Whether the camera finds more

If the CCTV shows a cracked or collapsed section, that's a repair, not a clear. I'll show you the footage and quote that separately — never a surprise on the invoice.

What Is Hydro Jetting — and Why It Costs More Than a Snake

Hydro jetting uses a high-pressure stream of water — around 5000 PSI — fired down the drain through a specialised nozzle. The jets blast forward to cut through the blockage and backward to drag the hose along and scour the pipe wall clean of grease, scale, sludge and roots. When it's done, the pipe is clear right to the wall, not just punched through the middle.

That's the difference from a drain snake, and it's why jetting usually costs a bit more. A snake bores a hole through the blockage and gets the water moving, but it leaves most of the gunk clinging to the pipe, so the drain blocks again before long. The jet strips the pipe clean. I've written up the full comparison in hydro jetting vs a drain snake — the short version is that a snake is cheaper on the day and a jet is cheaper over the year on anything stubborn or recurring.

Why a Camera Comes With the Jet

On every main-line job, a CCTV camera inspection is built into the price — not bolted on as an extra. There's a simple reason for that: jetting blind is guesswork, and guesswork costs you money. The camera goes down first so I can see exactly where the blockage is and what's causing it, then again afterwards to confirm the line is clear and show the condition of the pipe underneath.

That matters because the camera often changes the answer. If it shows roots coming through a cracked joint, a clear alone won't hold — you'll want to know that before you spend, not after. If it shows a collapsed section, no amount of jetting fixes a broken pipe, and the real solution is pipe relining. And if it shows a sound pipe that just had a grease build-up, great — you've got proof it's done and nothing more to spend. Either way you see the footage yourself. I'd rather show you what's down there and let you decide than sell you a fix you might not need.

Is Hydro Jetting Worth It — and How Often?

For a one-off minor clog, a standard clear might be all you need. Hydro jetting earns its keep when the blockage is stubborn, recurring, or caused by roots or heavy grease — the situations where a cheaper clear just comes back. On a main line that's prone to trouble, a jet clears it properly and buys you real time rather than a few weeks.

How often? If you've got big trees near the drain run or a history of blockages, a maintenance jet every year or two keeps a small build-up from turning into a weekend emergency. For most homes without those risk factors, you jet when there's a problem and don't think about it again. If roots are your issue, clearing alone is a cycle — the lasting fix is to seal the pipe, which I go through in the tree roots in drains guide. Not sure which camp you're in?
