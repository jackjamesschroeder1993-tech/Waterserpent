The Short Answer: It's About the Cause

Home insurance in Australia is built around one core idea — it covers things that are sudden and accidental, and excludes things that happen gradually or come down to a lack of maintenance. Almost every argument about whether a blocked drain is covered comes back to which side of that line your situation falls on.

A pipe that cracks and collapses out of nowhere, or a storm that overwhelms the system and forces water back up into the house, looks like a sudden event. A drain that's been slowly narrowing for years from fat, roots working into the joints, or old terracotta breaking down, looks like wear and tear. The blockage might feel sudden to you — one day it just stops draining — but insurers assess the underlying cause, and that's usually where blocked-drain claims are decided.

There's a second important distinction: the pipe and the blockage versus the damage it causes. Even where the pipe repair itself isn't covered, some policies cover resulting water damage to your floors, walls and contents under an "escape of liquid" benefit. So "am I covered?" can have two different answers on the same job.

When You're More Likely to Be Covered

No two policies are identical, but these are the situations where cover is worth investigating rather than writing off:

⚡ A sudden pipe collapse

If a section of pipe fails suddenly rather than degrading over years, it can look like an accidental event. A collapsed drain confirmed on camera is worth raising with your insurer.

🌧️ Storm and flood backup

Where a storm overwhelms the system and forces water back up, some policies with storm or flood cover may respond. Melbourne's spring and summer storms are the classic trigger. Check whether flood is included.

💧 Resulting water damage

Under "escape of liquid", damage to floors, walls and contents from a suddenly failed pipe may be covered even when the pipe repair itself is not. It's often the bigger cost, so it's worth checking.

🌳 Third-party or council trees

Where damage is caused by a tree that isn't yours — a neighbour's or a council street tree — liability can sit elsewhere. It's a separate path to an insurance claim, but worth exploring with your insurer or council.

In every one of these, the same thing decides it: evidence of what actually happened. Which is exactly why the next step for a serious blockage is a camera down the line, not a guess.

Why Blocked-Drain Claims Get Knocked Back

Two reasons come up again and again when a claim is refused. The first is that the damage gets classed as gradual or maintenance-related. Grease that built up over months, an old corroded pipe, roots that crept in slowly — these are the everyday causes of blocked drains in Melbourne, and they're also the textbook exclusions. It's frustrating, because the blockage genuinely felt sudden, but the assessor looks at the pipe, not the moment it stopped draining.

The second is a lack of evidence. If there's no plumber's report and no footage showing what caused the blockage and where it sits, the insurer has nothing to assess and often declines. Worse, if the drain has already been jetted clear before anyone documented it, the proof of the cause can be gone for good. This is the single most common own-goal: clearing first, documenting never.

Roots are the classic example. They're the number-one cause of blocked drains in the Dandenong Ranges and Yarra Valley — our guide on tree roots in drains explains why — and they're almost always treated as gradual damage. That doesn't automatically kill a claim for resulting water damage, but it does mean the paperwork has to be right.

What to Do If You Think You Can Claim

If your blocked drain has caused real damage and you think a claim is on the table, a few steps give you the best shot:

1. Make it safe first

If sewage is backing up inside or the home is flooding, stop the water and protect people and property straight away. Safety and stopping further damage come before paperwork — just photograph everything as you go.

2. Document before clearing

Where it's safe to wait, get a CCTV inspection to record the cause before the drain is jetted. Once it's cleared, the evidence of what caused it can be gone.

3. Keep every receipt & report

Save the plumber's report, the CCTV footage, invoices and dated photos. A written report without footage is harder to use — keep both together.

4. Read your PDS, then call your insurer

Check your Product Disclosure Statement for gradual-damage and flood exclusions, then talk to your insurer about their process — some want to approve an assessor before work begins.
