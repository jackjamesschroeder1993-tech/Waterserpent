Why Is My Kitchen Sink Draining Slowly? Causes, Fixes & When to Call a Plumber

July 2026 · 8 min read · By Jack Schroeder, Water Serpent Plumbing

Home›Blog›Kitchen Sink Draining Slowly

A kitchen sink that takes forever to empty is one of the most common jobs I see across Melbourne's east — and one of the most preventable. That pool of grey water sitting over the plughole while you wait isn't just annoying; it's the first, cheapest warning that grease and food are building up inside your pipe. Catch it now and it's a quick clear. Ignore it and you're heading for a fully blocked sink, a bad smell, and eventually water backing up over the bench.

This guide walks you through exactly why kitchen sinks drain slowly, what you can safely try yourself tonight, and the red flags that mean it's time to call a plumber rather than reach for another bottle of drain cleaner.

Why Kitchen Sinks Are the Worst for Slow Drainage

Of every drain in the house, the kitchen sink cops the hardest life. Everything that goes down it is exactly the stuff that builds up: cooking fat, oil, food scraps, coffee grounds, rice and soap. Hot fat pours away as a liquid, but the moment it hits the cooler pipe further along, it sets like candle wax on the walls. Every wash after that adds another thin layer, and scraps stick to it, until the channel that was the width of your fist is down to the width of a pencil.

That's why a kitchen sink rarely blocks overnight. It slows gradually over weeks — draining fine, then a little sluggish, then leaving a puddle — which is your cue to act before it stops completely.

"A slow kitchen sink is grease talking to you. It's the easiest blockage in the house to prevent and one of the messiest to leave — deal with it while it's still just slow."

The 5 Common Causes of a Slow-Draining Kitchen Sink

1. Grease and fat build-up (the big one)

By a mile the most common cause. Fats, oils and grease coat the inside of the pipe and trap everything else flowing past. It's why the classic advice — never pour cooking oil or bacon fat down the sink — matters so much. Wipe greasy pans with paper towel into the bin before washing, and you'll avoid most slow-sink call-outs entirely.

2. Food scraps and coffee grounds

Even with a strainer, small scraps slip through and lodge in the grease layer. Coffee grounds, tea leaves, rice and pasta are the worst — they clump and swell with water, and coffee grounds in particular sink and pack together into a dense sludge that water struggles to move.

3. A clogged P-trap

The P-trap is the U-bend of pipe directly under the sink. It's designed to hold a plug of water that blocks sewer smells, but its shape also makes it the first place gunk settles. A trap packed with grease and scraps is the most common single point of a slow kitchen drain — and often the easiest to clean out yourself with a bucket and a wrench.

4. A blocked or poorly vented drain line

If your sink drains slowly and gurgles, air is being trapped somewhere in the system. That can be a partial blockage further down the branch line, or a blocked vent stopping air entering the pipes the normal way. In the leafy Ranges and Yarra Valley suburbs, roof vents clog with leaves and debris more than most people realise. We break the noise down in our guide to why your drain is gurgling.

5. Tree roots or a deeper main-line blockage

If clearing the trap doesn't fix it, or the sink keeps slowing again and again, the trouble is deeper — often in the main drain the kitchen shares with other fixtures. In Melbourne's older established suburbs, tree roots creeping into cracked clay pipes are the number-one cause of recurring drain trouble. If the whole house is draining slowly, not just the kitchen, that's a main-line issue and a job for a camera. Our post on tree roots in drains covers this in depth.

One Sink or the Whole House? The Quick Test

Before you do anything, work out how much of the house is affected — it's the single most useful thing you can tell a plumber and it decides how worried to be.

If only the kitchen sink is slow, the problem is almost always local — grease in the trap or the pipe just past it. These are the ones you can often clear yourself.

If the kitchen sink is slow AND other fixtures are sluggish, gurgling or backing up — the bathroom basin bubbles, the laundry drains slowly, the toilet glugs — the blockage is deeper in the shared line. Plunging the kitchen sink won't touch it. That's your cue to stop experimenting and call a professional before it backs up.

What You Can Safely Try Yourself

If it's just the kitchen sink, a few simple steps are worth a go before you call anyone:

  • Flush with hot water. Boil the kettle, add a squirt of dish soap, and pour it slowly down the drain. The heat and detergent soften and loosen grease. Repeat weekly and it doubles as cheap maintenance.
  • Give it a proper plunge. Half-fill the sink so the plunger has water to work with, cover the overflow and the second plughole if it's a double sink, then plunge firmly a dozen times. This shifts a surprising amount of soft grease and scraps.
  • Clean out the P-trap. Put a bucket under the U-bend, unscrew the two slip nuts by hand or with a wrench, and clear the gunk inside. It's the highest-payoff DIY fix on a slow kitchen sink — just have towels ready.
  • Try baking soda and vinegar. Half a cup of baking soda, then half a cup of white vinegar, leave it to fizz for 15 minutes, then flush with hot water. It helps with light grease and odour, though it won't shift a real blockage further down.

One thing to avoid: supermarket chemical drain cleaners. They rarely clear a proper grease blockage, they don't touch tree roots, and the caustic chemicals can corrode older earthenware and PVC pipes — turning a cheap fix into an expensive one. If a bottle of caustic hasn't fixed it, the next plumber has to work around a pipe full of dangerous liquid too.

When to Call a Plumber

Pick up the phone if you notice any of these alongside the slow drainage:

  • The sink is still slow after you've cleaned the trap and flushed it
  • It keeps slowing down again within a week or two of clearing it
  • More than one fixture is draining slowly, gurgling or backing up
  • A sewage or rotten-egg smell coming from the drain
  • Water rising back up into the sink or another fixture

Any of these point to a blockage deeper in the line — and the longer it sits, the worse and pricier it gets. If it comes back within a few days, the blockage is further down the line and needs a plumber.

Slow drainage plus gurgling means air is being trapped by a blockage or a venting fault. Water forces past the obstruction and drags air with it, which you hear as gurgling.
