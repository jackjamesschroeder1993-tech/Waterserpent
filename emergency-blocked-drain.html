Is It a Drain Emergency? These Are the Signs

🚨 Sewage backing up inside

Waste rising in the shower, floor waste or lowest drain when you flush — stop using water immediately and call.

🛟 Overflowing gully trap

Waste water pooling around the outside grate near the house means the main line is fully blocked.

🚽 Toilet overflowing when flushed

One flush from flooding the bathroom — this is a blocked toilet emergency, and often a sign of a blocked sewer.

🏠 Every drain slow or blocked at once

Sinks, shower and toilet all struggling together points to the main sewer line, not one pipe.

🌧️ Stormwater flooding in rain

Water sheeting towards the house or garage because a stormwater drain can't cope — urgent in a downpour, especially in the Ranges.

What To Do Right Now (Before the Plumber Arrives)

1️⃣ Stop the water

No flushing, no taps, pause the washing machine and dishwasher — every litre has nowhere to go but back up.

2️⃣ Keep clear of the overflow

Raw sewage is a health hazard; keep kids and pets away and don't try to mop it into other drains.

3️⃣ Skip the chemical drain cleaner

It almost never clears a real blockage, and it turns the pipe into a hazard for whoever works on it.

How We Clear an Emergency Blocked Drain

Every emergency call-out runs the same proven process: a quick CCTV camera look to find the blockage and its cause, 5000 PSI hydro jetting to cut through tree roots, grease or debris and scour the pipe clean, then a final camera pass to prove the line is clear end-to-end. If the camera shows damaged pipe — cracked clay, root intrusion, a collapsed section — you'll see the footage yourself and get a fixed quote for a permanent fix like no-dig pipe relining.
