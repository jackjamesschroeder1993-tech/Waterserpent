Balwyn Sits on a Ridge — Water Leaves It Two Ways

Whitehorse Road runs east–west along the ridgeline through Balwyn. That one fact explains more blocked-drain calls in this suburb than anything else. Stormwater on the north side of the ridge has to find its way down to Koonung Creek; on the south side it heads for the Faversham Road and Balwyn main drains and eventually Back Creek country. A property a few doors either side of Whitehorse Road can be draining to a completely different system, and the first thing we establish on a Balwyn job is which way the water is supposed to be going — because when it is not going that way, it is sitting somewhere on your block.

Melbourne Water’s own list for Boroondara names three main drains that touch this suburb: the Balwyn Main Drain (Balwyn and Balwyn North), the Deepdene Main Drain (Balwyn, Balwyn North, Deepdene and Kew East) and the Faversham Road Main Drain (Balwyn, Canterbury and Surrey Hills). Your private pipes feed Council’s street drains, which feed those. The handover from private to public is what Boroondara calls the legal point of discharge, and the Council is clear that the owner is responsible for the stormwater system from inside the property to that point, including the pipes under the footpath to the kerb and channel. That footpath section surprises people. It is yours, and it is where a lot of Balwyn stormwater blockages actually sit.

Before the Van Arrives — Five Things Worth Doing

Most of the mess from a blocked drain is created in the half hour after it is noticed, not by the blockage itself. Short version: stop adding water, and go and look outside.

  1. Stop using water anywhere on the property, and tell everyone else to. A stopped sewer holds a fixed volume, and every extra litre lifts the level toward the lowest opening — which in an original Balwyn bungalow is usually the shower.
  2. Kill any appliance mid-cycle. A washing machine pump delivers a large slug of water in seconds.
  3. Find the overflow relief gully. It is the grated opening outside, often near the laundry or kitchen wall. If it is weeping or overflowing, the sewer is blocked downstream of it and it is doing its job. Do not seal it, and do not pave over it — we find that on renovated blocks more than you would think.
  4. Look for standing water in the yard and along the side path. On a Balwyn block the stormwater outlet often runs under the driveway to the footpath. A puddle that reappears after every ordinary shower of rain is the diagnostic.
  5. Leave the chemicals on the shelf. Caustic drain cleaner does nothing to roots and makes the pipe more dangerous to open. Our guide on whether drain cleaner works covers why.

The Balwyn Year, Season by Season

🍂 Autumn

Balwyn’s street trees are its whole character and its whole stormwater problem. Leaf load fills pits from the top down from March. The ones under the biggest street trees go first.

🌧️ Winter

Long steady rain removes whatever spare capacity a half-restricted stormwater line was relying on. Water at the base of a 1930s brick wall, or damp under a timber floor, starts turning up on the phone from June.

🌱 Spring

Root growth restarts. Any sewer cleared over winter without sealing the joint that let the roots in tends to close over again now, in the same metre of pipe as last time.

☀️ Summer

Short violent storm cells on a suburb that is mostly roof, driveway and hard paving. Baked ground sheds the lot straight into the pit network, and the restriction that coped all year shows itself in twenty minutes.

Warning Signs on a Balwyn Property

  • One fixture answering another — flush and the basin burps, run the bath and the shower rises. Our guide on gurgling drains explains what is happening.
  • The same puddle after every ordinary shower of rain — repetition is the diagnostic. Big storms move about; a restriction stays put.
  • An outdoor grate lifting or overflowing — the overflow relief gully operating as designed. Urgent, not cosmetic.
  • A drain that was “fixed” last year doing it again — the symptom was cleared, the cause was not. Our guide on why drains keep blocking is written for exactly this.
  • A green stripe in the lawn, or a patch that never dries — a cracked sewer feeding the garden. Common where a 1930s line runs under a now-mature tree.
  • A new build that blocks in its first year — almost always the new PVC tied into an old council stub or an old house drain that was left in the ground. See the FAQ below.

3️⃣ Run the camera over the cleared length

Once water is moving the camera goes down, and what it records is the finished bore plus the condition of the pipe either side of the trouble. That recording belongs to you.

4️⃣ Say plainly what it showed

A root entry, a dropped joint, a new line badly tied into an old one — whatever the camera found gets described straight, including the cases where leaving it for now is the honest advice. Relining is the permanent answer when it is not.

When a Balwyn Line Needs Relining, Not Clearing

Balwyn was sewered by 1927 and most of the suburb was built in the decade after. That is a lot of earthenware approaching its century, laid in short lengths with joints that were never designed to hold out the root system of a mature street tree. Clearing the roots restores flow; it does not close the joint they came through. If the camera shows the same open joint being re-entered year after year, a liner cured inside the existing pipe seals it for good, without digging up the garden that caused the problem. The call is yours, made from the footage, with a written quote first. Our relining versus replacement guide walks through the trade-offs.

Three Main Drains, Two Creeks, and None of It Visible

Boroondara says it has 48 kilometres of waterways, but you would not know it from Balwyn. Both creeks that bracket the suburb have been put underground. Koonung Creek, on the north side, is piped within Boroondara to the 1% AEP level with only a small overland flow path, and most of its catchment sits outside the municipality altogether. When the Eastern Freeway’s second stage went through in 1982, 2.4 kilometres of the creek were placed underground, and the Koonung Creek Reserve that replaced it is now being reshaped again by the North East Link works. Council adopted a master plan for the reserve in February 2024 that keeps the existing wetland and rejected the project’s proposal for additional stormwater swales and ponds there.

Glass Creek rises near Loma Linda Grove in Greythorn and runs through Jacka Street, Gordon Barnard Reserve, Hislop Park and the Macleay and Myrtle parks on its way to the Yarra — mostly in underground drains, the downstream sections piped in the 1950s with a tender let in 1964, and only about 850 metres still open in Hays Paddock. Its local catchment is around 5 square kilometres, and the emergency plan notes that high Yarra levels can back water up Glass Creek and the Kew Main Drain.

For a Balwyn property the practical point is this: the creek your stormwater is heading for is a pipe. It has a design capacity, it is shared with thousands of other properties, and when it is full your private drain cannot discharge no matter how clean it is. That is the difference between a blockage, which we fix, and a capacity problem, which nobody fixes in an afternoon — and the camera is what tells the two apart.

Boroondara’s Flood Picture, and Where Balwyn Sits in It

The Council’s 2016 flood mapping study covered every catchment in Boroondara and identified around 5,800 properties at risk of flash flooding in rare rainfall events — modelled on the 1% AEP storm, meaning overland flow from drains that cannot cope, not creek flooding. Properties inside that mapping carry a Special Building Overlay (stormwater) or a Land Subject to Inundation Overlay (watercourses), both of which need a planning permit, and show up flagged as “subject to flooding under Building Regulation 153” on a Property Information Statement. Finished floors in those areas must sit above the flood level plus 300 millimetres of freeboard. The flood assessment report itself is free; a flood level approval is $310. Council says it is currently updating its flood modelling across Boroondara.

It is worth being straight about what the record does and does not say. VICSES publishes local flood guides for Boroondara only for Glen Iris and Hawthorn — Balwyn, Deepdene and Balwyn North are not named in them, and we have found no list of flood-prone Balwyn streets. The municipal emergency plan’s historic floods are 1891 (the worst on record), 1914, 1934, 1974, December 2003, December 2004, December 2010 and the 4–5 February 2011 event when Kew recorded 99 millimetres in 24 hours, with “Eastern Freeway” among the locations that have flooded. The December 2016 storm brought the SES more than 1,200 calls across the municipality. None of that makes Balwyn a floodplain. It makes it a suburb whose piped creeks fill up in the big events, which is when a half-blocked private line stops being an inconvenience.

Sewered by 1927 — What Is Under the Bungalows

The Whitehorse Road tram reached Mont Albert on 30 September 1916 and was through-routed to the city in 1929; sewerage reached Balwyn by 1927 and North Balwyn in 1938. In between, the farms and orchards were cut up through the late 1920s and 30s, and the block bounded by Whitehorse, Belmore, Burke and Balwyn Roads filled with the Californian Bungalows and English Revival houses that define the suburb, a good many of them timber bungalows built on State Savings Bank credit foncier loans. The Reid Estate went in north of Whitehorse Road in the 1930s, and around 200 red-brick Housing Commission homes followed in the late 1940s.

Since the 1990s a large share of those interwar houses have been replaced — the Balwyn High School zone, where properties inside the catchment sell for hundreds of thousands more than those outside it, has driven a lot of that turnover. For drains, the result is a suburb of three ages laid on top of each other: 1920s–30s earthenware house drains at their design life, mid-century repairs and extensions, and brand-new PVC on rebuilt blocks that still connects to a council stub from the first era. The camera is the only reliable way to know which of the three you are dealing with, and it is the first thing down the pipe on any Balwyn job that has blocked more than once.
